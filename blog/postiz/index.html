The founder's moves8 moves

    The two moves that mattered.

    Everything else on the timeline supports these two.

    Move 1

    Free and open source, as the way people found it

    Anyone can run Postiz free on their own server. People who don't want the hassle pay $29+ a month for the hosted version. Every small feature shipped as a new release, and every release was a reason to post on Hacker News, Reddit and Dev.to.

    "Charge only for cloud costs, never force developers to pay."

    Claimed · founder post, Nov 2025
    Move 2

    Plugging into the AI agent wave early

    Postiz shipped an n8n integration, then an MCP server, a command-line tool and an OpenClaw skill, so AI agents could post through Postiz by default. When OpenClaw went viral in early 2026 and someone else's article about it used Postiz, signups jumped. Revenue grew from $21K to $118K a month in about three months.

    Claimed · Superframeworks, Mixergy

    The story, phase by phase.

    Each phase ends with one lesson you can use.

    0Before Postiz2022 to 2023

    The founder was a growth marketer at Novu, another open-source company, where the project grew to 30,000 GitHub stars in two years. His first solo product, Gitroom, made some money but "the market was tiny". He dropped it after about six months.

    Start where you already understand how people find things. Drop a product quickly when the market is too small.

    1, 2BuildingJan to Aug 2024

    Postiz started as a regular social media scheduler for creators, businesses and agencies. He spent about $12,000 on SEO articles for a brand-new website. They brought almost no traffic, and he stopped.

    July 2024 homepage: "Your ultimate social media scheduling tool." Nothing that stood out from Buffer or Hootsuite yet. Claimed

    A new domain has no authority with Google. Paying for SEO in month one is usually money gone.

    3Open sourceSep 2024 to 2025

    He open-sourced it and ran launch weeks on Hacker News, Reddit, Dev.to and Product Hunt to reach GitHub's trending page. Reddit users asked for a Docker setup, so he built one. GitHub stars went from about 3K to 14K in three months.

    Revenue went from about $700 a month to $6.5K by July 2025 and $14K by November 2025. Over a year of slow growth. Claimed

    Free gets attention, but attention is not revenue. Expect a long stretch where the free version grows and the paid version barely moves.

    4, 5Finding the users who stay2025

    He noticed people using Postiz inside n8n automations and YouTube tutorials about automating social media. He published an official n8n node, improved the public API, messaged Skool communities to promote Postiz, and started an affiliate program paying about 30% every month for as long as the customer stays.

    His reasoning: people posting by hand cancel, but "when people are posting with automation, that's a whole different story."

    Look at who your most active users are, then build for them.

    6The turning pointEarly 2026

    OpenClaw, an AI agent project, went viral. Postiz was ready with an MCP server, a command-line tool and an OpenClaw skill. A third-party writer published a popular article about an agent that posted TikTok slideshows through Postiz.

    Reported: $21K a month in March 2026, $80K in April, $118K in June, $145K on July 2. Claimed

    You can't plan a viral moment, but you can be the tool that's ready when one happens.

    7, 8Scaling the contentSep 2026 to now

    A weekly system on X: a Monday video that is useful on its own and only mentions Postiz, clips of it all week, then the same idea as a long X article the next week. He replies to every comment, and runs daily short videos through creators, where he says about 1 in 30 takes off.

    Reported $186,713 a month in September 2026. Claimed $430,896 a month on November 17, 2026. Verified

    One idea, repackaged several times, beats new content every day.
