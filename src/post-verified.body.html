What 'Verified' means on LaunchVault

Where our revenue numbers come from, why some are labelled Claimed, and why every number has a date.

ExplainersLaunchVault6 min readNov 17 2026
In short
  • Verified means we read the number from a live source, like Stripe through TrustMRR, on the date shown.
  • Claimed means a founder or an interview said it. We link to where, but we couldn't check it.
  • A verified number can go stale. If the source stops updating, we say so and show the last date.

Where the numbers come from

Most revenue numbers you see online are screenshots. They can be cropped, rounded or edited, and you can't tell. LaunchVault starts from a different place: TrustMRR, a site that connects to a company's payment account with a read-only key and reads the revenue straight from Stripe or RevenueCat.

When a number on a breakdown comes from there, we label it Verified and add the date we read it.

The six labels

  • Verified read from a live source on the date shown.
  • Claimed said by the founder or in an interview. We link to where they said it.
  • Sources differ two sources disagree. We show both and say which one the chart uses.
  • Live refreshed from the source itself, like GitHub stars, with the time we read it.
  • Wayback a dated snapshot of a page from the Internet Archive.
  • Couldn't verify a claim with no source either way. We say so instead of guessing.

A number is only as good as where it came from, and when.

Why every number has a date

A connection can stop updating. On TrustMRR, Gojiberry's page was last updated on August 14, 2026, so on LaunchVault its $424,368 MRR carries that date, not today's.

Numbers also move every day. Postiz showed $423,707 MRR on November 16, 2026 and $430,896 a day later, so each one keeps the date it was read. Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) also isn't the same as all the money that came in, so we say which one a number is.
