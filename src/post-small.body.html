Three ways to start small

A family member's problem, a service you can sell now, or a free version people can try. A real example of each, and one thing to try this week.

1. A problem someone close to you has

Stencil AI is a mobile app that turns a photo into a tattoo stencil. One brother ran into the problem at a tattoo shop; the other could code. They had 16 followers on X, and the app has made $483,794 in total Verified.

The move to copy isn't "build an AI app". It's that the problem came from someone they could ask questions every day.

This week: ask three people in your family what wastes their time at work. Write the answers down.

2. A service you can sell now

Windmill Growth is a LinkedIn personal-branding agency for founders. It started with $250, the founder says Claimed. Today it has 74 clients paying about $1,600 a month each, $118,347 a month in total Verified.

A service earns before an app would, because you sell your time and skill first.

You don't need thousands of users if each one pays properly.

This week: offer to run a local shop's Instagram for a month, for a small fee.

3. A free version people can try

Postiz is free to run on your own server and paid if you want the hosted version, from $29 a month, the same starting price as in 2024. Every release was a reason to post on Hacker News, Reddit and Dev.to.

Free took a long time to pay off. Postiz stayed under $15K a month for about 14 months after going open source Claimed. Today it makes $430,896 a month Verified.

This week: ship one small thing, then post it where your users already are.

What not to copy

The one big early spend we know about didn't work. Postiz paid about $12,000 for SEO articles on a brand-new website and got almost no traffic Claimed. Write a few useful posts yourself instead.
